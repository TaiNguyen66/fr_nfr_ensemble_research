\begin{abstract}
Studies on classifying functional (FR) and non-functional requirements (NFR) report ever higher scores, some reaching 98--99\%. Common evaluation choices can make such estimates optimistic: random splits that ignore duplicates and projects, oversampling before splitting, and ensemble claims without statistical tests. We measured their effect on PROMISE\_exp (969 requirements, 47 projects), with a replication on the data of Dalpiaz et al. Six classifiers, including hard voting and stacking, were evaluated with TF-IDF features under stratified, duplicate-grouped and project-grouped 5-fold CV, repeated with 5 seeds. Differences were judged with the corrected repeated $k$-fold $t$-test and Holm adjustment, following a protocol locked before any run. Oversampling before splitting inflated macro-F1 on the 12-class task by 0.39 to 0.49 (to 0.95--0.99), whereas on the binary task the difference was not clear. Testing on unseen projects lowered macro-F1 by 0.13 to 0.19 and changed the model ranking. Grouping duplicates showed no clear difference on this dataset, and ensembles were clearly better only than random forest. We recommend oversampling inside training folds only, reporting duplicate handling and project-level results, and using corrected tests before claiming that one model is better.
\end{abstract}
