\begin{abstract}
Studies on classifying functional (FR) and non-functional requirements (NFR) report ever higher scores, some reaching 98--99\%. Common evaluation choices can make such estimates optimistic: random splits that ignore duplicates and projects, oversampling before splitting, and ensemble claims without statistical tests. We measured their effect on PROMISE\_exp (969 requirements, 47 projects), with a replication on the data of Dalpiaz et al. Six classifiers and a dummy baseline, including hard voting and stacking, were evaluated with TF-IDF features under stratified, duplicate-grouped and project-grouped 5-fold CV, repeated with 5 seeds. Model comparisons on the same splits were judged with the corrected repeated $k$-fold $t$-test and Holm adjustment, and differences between protocols were reported descriptively, following a protocol locked before any run. Oversampling before splitting inflated macro-F1 on the 12-class task by 0.39 to 0.49 (to 0.95--0.99), whereas on the binary task the increase was small (0.02--0.03). Testing on unseen projects lowered macro-F1 by 0.13 to 0.19 and changed the model ranking. Grouping duplicates changed macro-F1 by less than 0.01 on this dataset, and ensembles were clearly better only than random forest. We recommend oversampling inside training folds only, reporting duplicate handling and project-level results, and using corrected tests before claiming that one model is better.
\end{abstract}
